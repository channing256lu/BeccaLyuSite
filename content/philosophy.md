## Understanding Growth

### Section label

01. Understanding Growth

### Growth title

Growth is Development and Ongoing

### Growth statement

Children’s physical, social, emotional, linguistic and cognitive development unfolds through interconnected stages and does not always progress in a simple or linear way. Knowledge of developmental milestones helps me recognise emerging capabilities, understand what may be developmentally appropriate and provide timely support. However, milestones should guide professional observation rather than operate as rigid deadlines or labels. A child who has not yet demonstrated a skill should not automatically be viewed as unable; their learning may require further time, experience, intentional teaching or a different form of support. Respecting development therefore means neither rushing children nor passively waiting, but responding thoughtfully to what is emerging.

### Reference

(ACECQA, 2012, 2024; Australian Government Department of Education, 2022)

### Development title

No Flower Must Become a Rose

### Development premise

No child needs to become the same kind of flower to be recognised as capable.

### Development comparison

- A rose, a jasmine and a dandelion grow differently and contribute in different ways, yet each has its own value.
- Likewise, children may flourish in different areas, develop at different paces and follow different pathways.

### Development paragraph

Their capabilities may also become visible through different ways of thinking, communicating and expressing understanding. The Reggio Emilia image of the child as capable, rights-bearing and rich in “a hundred languages” reminds me that children make meaning through words, drawing, movement, music, play, construction, gesture and relationships (Edwards et al., 2011).

### Development highlight

Every child is precious and worthy of being seen for who they are and who they are becoming.

### Knowing title

Knowing Each Child

### Knowing subtitle

Knowing begins with attention, not assumption.

### Knowing intro

Responsive teaching begins with knowing each child as an individual and as a member of their family, culture and community.

### Knowing paragraphs

- Through observation, conversation, documentation and respectful partnerships with families, I seek to understand children’s interests, strengths, prior experiences, identities and emerging learning needs.
- Children’s interests offer meaningful entry points into learning, while careful observation helps me recognise what they already know, what they are beginning to understand and where further possibilities may lie.
- However, interests should not become fixed labels or limit children’s access to new knowledge and experiences; we also need to extend their thinking and broaden their possibilities.

### Education reference

(Australian Government Department of Education, 2022)

### Conditions title

Creating Supportive Conditions

### Conditions paragraphs

- Just as a flower needs fertile soil in which to grow, children need safe, responsive and supportive environments in which they can learn and flourish.
- My role is not to determine what each child should become, but to cultivate the conditions that make growth possible. Through trusting relationships, emotional safety, a strong sense of belonging and predictable learning environments, I aim to help children feel secure enough to explore new ideas, express themselves and engage confidently in learning.

### Conditions highlight

When engagement is difficult, I consider whether the language, pace, space, task or expectations may be creating barriers.

### Quote section label

### Scaffolding title

Responsive Scaffolding

### Scaffolding subtitle

Support should be responsive, not identical.

### Scaffolding paragraphs

- Depending on the child and the learning purpose, I may use modelling, visual prompts, oral rehearsal, movement, questioning, feedback or additional processing time.
- Child-centred practice does not mean that the educator becomes passive. Intentional teaching requires professional judgement about when to model, when to extend thinking, when to provide support and when to step back so that children can develop confidence, independence and agency.

## Learning Before Bloom

### Title

Learning Before Bloom

### Intro

Learning becomes visible long before the final bloom.

### Progress title

Making Progress Visible

### Progress subtitle

Learning becomes visible long before the final bloom

### Progress paragraphs

- I use ongoing observation, conversations, documentation and work samples to notice not only what children achieve, but also how they think, solve problems, communicate, persist and collaborate throughout the learning process.
- Formative assessment helps me identify emerging understanding and plan meaningful next steps, while summative assessment provides evidence of achievement against relevant learning expectations. Together, process and outcome evidence create a more complete picture of children’s progress.

### Assessment note

Professional dialogue and moderation help me question my assumptions and develop fairer, more consistent and comparable judgements.

### Feedback section label

02 · Feedback for the Next Step

### Feedback title

Feedback should open the next possibility.

### Feedback paragraphs

- Feedback should help children understand both what they have achieved and what they can do next. Rather than relying on general praise, I aim to recognise specific strategies, decisions, persistence and improvement.
- Encouragement should build confidence, but it should also remain purposeful by guiding children towards new strategies, deeper thinking or greater independence.

### Feedback reference

(Hattie & Timperley, 2007; Australian Government Department of Education, 2022)

### Before feedback title

General praise

### Before feedback quote

“Good job!”

### Before feedback note

Encouragement without detail leaves the next step unclear.

### Better feedback title

Purposeful feedback

### Better feedback quote

“You tried two different strategies before you found one that worked. What helped you decide?”

### Better feedback note

Specific feedback returns thinking to the child.

Growth Alongside Children

### Final paragraph

My teaching philosophy also requires me to reflect on my own growth as an educator. I use children’s responses, mentor feedback, professional dialogue and research to question my assumptions and refine my practice. I understand that effective teaching is never finished; it develops through observation, reflection and a continuing commitment to children’s learning, wellbeing and rights.
