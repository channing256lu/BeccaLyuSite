# From Feedback to Professional Growth

Using video reflection to improve phonemic awareness teaching.

Date: 1 May–5 June 2026

Setting: Reception classroom, Immanuel Primary School

## C — Context

As part of my professional improvement plan, I identified literacy teaching as a priority area for growth, particularly phonemic awareness instruction (refer to Figure 1). For my first formal Heggerty lesson during Week 1 of my Reception placement, I asked my mentor to record the session so I could review my practice from the children’s perspective. After watching the video, I identified concerns with mirrored gestures, pacing, transitions and classroom routines. I documented these areas for improvement (refer to Figure 2) and then discussed my proposed strategies with my mentor to confirm that they were appropriate and practical for the class.

[Figure 1. Professional Learning Planner (PLP): Identifying areas for improvement in literacy teaching.](https://pub-0d63e00396f84c818d577fbc98732ebd.r2.dev/ProfessionalEngagement/Evidence1/file/Figure1.pdf)

[Figure 2. Video-Based Self-Reflection: Identifying weaknesses and planning next steps.](https://pub-0d63e00396f84c818d577fbc98732ebd.r2.dev/ProfessionalEngagement/Evidence1/file/Figure2.docx)

## A — Action

Using my self-reflection and mentor feedback as a guide, I developed and implemented a targeted improvement plan for subsequent Heggerty lessons. I practised correct pronunciation and gestures, shortened teacher prompts, clarified whiteboard routines and checked that all children were ready before transitions. To strengthen participation, I introduced a hedgehog mascot, a daily “Heggerty Hero” leadership role (refer to Figure 3) and a visual class participation system (refer to Figure 4), alongside specific feedback and calm redirection.

[Figure 3. Engaging Phonemic Awareness Learning: Introducing Heggerty the Hedgehog and Heggerty Heroes.](https://pub-0d63e00396f84c818d577fbc98732ebd.r2.dev/ProfessionalEngagement/Evidence1/image/Figure3.jpg)

[Figure 4. Classroom Behaviour Management: Introducing a class pet reward system.](https://pub-0d63e00396f84c818d577fbc98732ebd.r2.dev/ProfessionalEngagement/Evidence1/image/Figure4.png)

## R — Results

In Week 2, university liaison feedback recognised clear preparation, varied teaching strategies, positive relationships, non-verbal communication, proactive monitoring and improved participation (refer to Figure 5). The routine remained successful across the following five weeks, while a firmer teacher voice remained an ongoing goal.

[Figure 5. Feedback on Improved Teaching Practice: University liaison feedback following classroom observation.](https://pub-0d63e00396f84c818d577fbc98732ebd.r2.dev/ProfessionalEngagement/Evidence1/file/Figure5.pdf)

## E — Evaluation

This experience showed me that engagement tools are effective only when supported by accurate content knowledge, clear communication and consistent routines. I also recognised that external rewards should support, rather than replace, children’s agency, belonging and intrinsic participation.
